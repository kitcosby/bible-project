# Acts 15:32 — variant-dependent alignment of “also and”

**Status:** One unresolved Greek token remains. The stable καί → “and” link is proposed; the particle at position 2 remains unresolved. No production approval.

## Primary text observation

The YLT token sequence is:

> Judas also and Silas, being themselves also prophets ...

In the YLT-profile Greek candidate sequence, the corresponding opening is:

> Ἰούδας δὲ καὶ Σίλας

The first “also” (YLT token 2) is distinct from the later “also” (YLT token 7), which corresponds to the second καί in the phrase καὶ αὐτοὶ προφῆται.

## Robinson variant provenance

The Robinson source parser records variant group **44:19** with two normalized branches:

| Branch | Greek payload | Morphology |
|---|---|---|
| A | τε (G5037) | particle |
| B | δέ (G1161) | conjunction |

The Robinson source profile remains **unselected**. The CrossWire/KJV candidate sequence selected branch B algorithmically. The YLT candidate sequence inherited branch B provisionally from that KJV candidate; this is explicitly not a YLT editorial decision.

Thus the current YLT-profile sequence displays δέ, but the sequence provenance does not establish that Young’s translation was made from branch B.

## External textual and grammatical evidence

- [OpenBible’s Acts 15:32 Greek comparison](https://www.openbible.com/text/acts/15-32.htm) displays Nestle 1904 with Ἰούδας τε καὶ Σιλᾶς and Scrivener’s Textus Receptus with Ἰούδας δὲ καὶ Σίλας. Its interlinear glosses τε as “both” and καί as “and” in the Nestle reading.
- The [UBS Dictionary / MACULA clause analysis](https://bibleontology.org/p/Acts.15.32) treats the τε καί phrase as “Judas both and Silas”. That analysis belongs to the τε branch, not the Robinson B reading.
- The [Scrivener TR interlinear](https://bible.4disciples.com/Acts/15/32) leaves δέ without a separate gloss and glosses the following καί as “and”. It also gives δέ a broad range including continuative and additive renderings, sometimes unexpressed.
- Robertson, *A Grammar of the Greek New Testament* (1919), pp. 1184–1185, discusses δέ as both copulative/continuative and adversative, and notes δὲ καί examples where the discourse advances in the same direction. This establishes that δέ need not mean “but”; it does not prove that δέ independently supplies YLT “also” here. [Grammar text](https://www.ibiblio.org/bgreek/project/robertson/Robertson_GreekGrammar.pdf).

Correction to the earlier draft: Beza 1598 supports A=τε, not B=δέ. Stephanus 1550 and Beza 1598 therefore agree on A; Elzevir 1624 and Scrivener 1894 support B. Scrivener 1894 postdates Young's death (1888) and cannot itself have been Young's printed exemplar. It is useful as a later reconstruction of the Greek text associated with the KJV, not direct evidence of Young's own edition.

Young's preface says he followed the Received Text generally, but does not identify a specific edition. The YLT's opening “Judas also and Silas” is more naturally compatible with τε καί: τε contributes the additive/correlative force and καί introduces Silas. This is reinforced by the fact that both Stephanus 1550 and Beza 1598—pre-1888 Received Text editions—read τε. The phrase is not mathematically decisive, since δέ can function continuatively and translation syntax is not a manuscript witness. However, the combined evidence now favors A over B for the YLT profile.

## Alignment decision

1. **καί (Greek position 3) → YLT “and” (token 3):** stable across both variant branches and proposed with high confidence.
2. **Position 2 (branch A τε / branch B δέ) → YLT “also” (token 2):** propose branch A (τε), with moderate confidence. The YLT's literal “Judas also and Silas” is a better fit for τε καί, and Stephanus 1550 and Beza 1598 both support A. Keep the proposal distinct from a final edition identification: Young's exact exemplar remains unknown.
3. The later καί (Greek position 5) → YLT “also” (token 7) remains a separate existing association.

The shadow phrase group AC-15:32-de-kai-additive records the two-token/two-token context. It does not force the unresolved particle into a one-to-one lexical gloss. The stable καί link remains proposed. Branch A is now a YLT-specific research proposal; do not silently treat the inherited KJV branch B as authoritative. The shadow database selection and dependent alignment row have not yet been changed.

## Evidence classification

- **Established:** Robinson variant group 44:19 has A=τε and B=δέ; Robinson source selection is unselected; YLT candidate B is inherited provisionally from the KJV candidate.
- **Established:** the YLT wording is “Judas also and Silas”; the stable καί at position 3 corresponds to “and” at YLT token 3.
- **Imported grammatical evidence:** δέ can be continuative/copulative as well as adversative; τε καί can express a “both … and” relation.
- **Verified derivation / moderate-confidence proposal:** YLT “Judas also and Silas” is better explained by branch A τε καί than branch B δὲ καί, especially given Stephanus 1550 and Beza 1598.
- **Unresolved:** Young's exact printed Greek exemplar; the evidence favors A but does not identify a single edition.

No YLT-specific variant decision is inferred from the KJV candidate. Branch A is recommended for the YLT profile as a proposal, not a final textual verdict. The isolated shadow database remains unchanged pending review of the dependent alignment row and validator impact.
