# Ezekiel 27:32 — medieval Hebrew lexical reception note (2026-11-08)

## Purpose

This note records a focused check of medieval Hebrew interpretation of MT בְּנִיהֶם. It supplements the textual-witness audit but does not add manuscript witnesses or settle the lexical derivation.

## Findings in Mikra'ot Gedolot HaKeter

The online HaKeter presentation for Ezekiel 27:32 includes several medieval explanations:

- **Rashi:** explains בניהם as בנְהִי שלהם — “their נהי,” i.e. their lament/wailing. This is an interpretation of the received MT form, not evidence for a different manuscript reading.
- **Radak:** says the expression is “a matter of נהי,” while noting that, according to the grammarians, it is a different root; Radak then offers his own morphological explanation involving a yod corresponding to a heh in the root. This demonstrates that the derivation was already disputed among medieval Hebrew grammarians.
- **Earlier grammatical discussion:** the HaKeter note reports earlier grammatical proposals concerning the form and its relation to root structure. Because the display combines material from more than one commentator, this addendum does not assign every sentence to a named author without checking the underlying critical edition.
- **Malbim:** explicitly relates בניהם to נהי, explaining the lament as a call-and-response lamentation: one person raises a lament and the hearers answer with נהי and הוי.

## Evidential significance

This reception evidence strengthens the conclusion that reading MT בְּנִיהֶם as a mourning expression is not a modern rescue hypothesis. It was a recognized interpretation in medieval Jewish exegesis and grammatical discussion.

It does **not** prove:
- that נִי is an independently established Hebrew noun;
- that the exact derivation is contraction/syncope from נְהִי;
- that the MT reading is earlier than בְּנֵיהֶם;
- or that medieval commentators had access to an independent Hebrew textual witness.

Keep separate:
1. the **semantic interpretation** “lament/wailing,” which has substantial reception support;
2. the **morphological/etymological explanation**, which remains disputed;
3. the **textual-critical question** between MT בְּנִיהֶם and the alternative “their sons.”

## Disposition

No change to the adjudication status: **OPEN_TEXT_CRITICAL_REVIEW**. Preserve OSHB/WLC בְּנִיהֶם, H5204, morphology, YLT wording, alignments, and all production data. This is an interpretive-history note, not a source correction.
