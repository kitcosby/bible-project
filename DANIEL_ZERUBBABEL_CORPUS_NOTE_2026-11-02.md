## Daniel's place and role — what the corpus shows

- In this corpus's book order Daniel is book 27, between Ezekiel (26) and
  Hosea (28) — the Christian/prophetic placement. In the Hebrew canon Daniel
  sits in the Ketuvim (Writings), not the Prophets — the "remembered different"
  discrepancy Kit flagged in his first message.
- Language split, counted from the words table: Daniel has 2,084 Hebrew words
  and 3,304 Aramaic words. By chapter: ch. 1 Hebrew; ch. 2 Aramaic from v.4
  (39 Hebrew words remain in the chapter head); chs. 3–7 Aramaic; chs. 8–12
  Hebrew. So the book is majority-Aramaic — Daniel the courtier/seer of the
  Aramaic tales is the larger half of the book, Daniel the Hebrew apocalyptic
  visionary the smaller.
- Ezekiel names a Daniel three times: Ezek 14:14, 14:20 (with Noah and Job,
  as a paragon of righteousness) and Ezek 28:3 ("thou art wiser than Daniel").
  In Ezekiel the consonantal spelling is the short form דנאל (Dan'el) — the
  corpus carries both that spelling and the pointed דָּנִיֵּאל under Strong's
  H1840. Ezekiel's Daniel reads as an ancient figure of proverbial
  righteousness/wisdom, not Ezekiel's contemporary in exile.

## Belshazzar — the "his son" problem, Babylon side

- Belshazzar appears in exactly 8 verses, all in Daniel: 5:1, 5:2, 5:9, 5:22,
  5:29, 5:30, 7:1, 8:1.
- Daniel calls Nebuchadnezzar his father: Dan 5:2, 5:11, 5:18 name
  Nebuchadnezzar in ch. 5, and 5:22 addresses Belshazzar directly: "And thou
  his son, O Belshazzar, hast not humbled thine heart…" Historically
  Belshazzar was son of Nabonidus, last king of Babylon, acting as regent —
  "son" in Daniel is dynastic language. (Historical half is standard reference
  knowledge, not a corpus claim.)

## Zerubbabel — the "his son" problem, Judah side, and the signet reversal

- Zerubbabel appears in exactly 21 verses: 1 Chr 3:19; Ezra 2:2, 3:2, 3:8,
  4:2, 4:3, 5:2; Neh 7:7, 12:1, 12:47; Hag 1:1, 1:12, 1:14, 2:2, 2:4, 2:21,
  2:23; Zech 4:6, 4:7, 4:9, 4:10.
- Two fathers on the record, inside the corpus itself:
  - "Son of Shealtiel" everywhere in Ezra/Nehemiah/Haggai (e.g. Ezra 3:2,
    Hag 1:1, and Hag 2:23 itself).
  - 1 Chr 3:19: "And the sons of Pedaiah were, Zerubbabel, and Shimei…"
    The genealogy in 1 Chr 3:17–19 runs Jeconiah → Shealtiel (3:17) → his
    brother Pedaiah (3:18, listed among the next generation's sons) →
    Zerubbabel as Pedaiah's son (3:19). Levirate/adoption is the usual
    harmonization; the corpus simply carries both statements.
- The signet reversal — the strongest "darling" link, both ends in the corpus:
  - Jer 22:24, to his grandfather: "though Coniah the son of Jehoiakim king
    of Judah were the signet upon my right hand, yet would I pluck thee
    thence" — the Davidic signet torn off.
  - Hag 2:23, to Zerubbabel: "will I take thee, O Zerubbabel, my servant,
    the son of Shealtiel, saith the LORD, and will make thee as a signet:
    for I have chosen thee" — the same image, restored to the grandson.
  - Zech 4:9–10 adds the builder's half: "The hands of Zerubbabel have laid
    the foundation of this house; his hands shall also finish it."
  After Haggai/Zechariah he vanishes from the record and no crown follows —
  the hope pinned on him is exactly what makes him "the darling" of the
  restoration generation, and exactly the kind of remembered-vs-recorded
  gap Kit was circling.
