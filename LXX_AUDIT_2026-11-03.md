# LXX source audit — CenterBLC/LXX (2026-11-03)

Second step of Kit's sequence ("Gloss fix first, then LXX audit"; the
gloss fix shipped the same day — see NOTATION_LEDGER.md §10
GLOSS-NUMBER). The dataset was cloned and **loaded and censused here**
(tf-venv, Text-Fabric), not just read about. Clone kept at
/tmp/lxx-audit (audit only — nothing has entered the corpus).

## The candidate

**CenterBLC/LXX** — Rahlfs' 1935 Septuagint in Text-Fabric format, the
same pipeline family as our SP, DSS, and Peshitta corpora.
Load: `use("CenterBLC/LXX", version="1935")`. Repo license: **MIT**.
Repo status: WIP badge; last updated 2026-08.

## Census (measured on the loaded dataset)

- **57 books** (OSIS codes), **30,371 verses**, **623,693 words**,
  **13,943 distinct lexemes**.
- Canon: the full Greek canon, including everything with no MT
  counterpart — 1/2 Esdras, Judith, Tobit (**two recensions**: TobBA,
  TobS), 1–4 Maccabees, Wisdom, Sirach, Baruch + Epistle of Jeremiah,
  Psalms of Solomon, Odes, Psalm 151 — and **double traditions** for
  Daniel material: Dan (Old Greek) vs DanTh (Theodotion), Susanna and
  Bel likewise split (Sus/SusTh, Bel/BelTh). Esther has 10 chapters
  (the Greek additions are integrated).
- **Strong's G numbers on 581,768 / 623,693 words (93.3%)** — despite
  the README listing Strong's-to-BHSA matching as future work, a
  `strongs` feature exists and is populated.
- **Lemma gloss on 100% of words** (`gloss`, e.g. ἀρχῇ → "origin;
  beginning"); BibleOL glosses (`bol_gloss`) on 536,471 (86.0%).
- **`translit_SBL` on 100% of words** — the Greek side would have the
  transliteration our Hebrew corpus lacks.
- **Full morphology decomposition**: sp (part of speech), person,
  gender, number, tense, mood, voice, case, degree + the raw CATSS
  morphology code — a richer parse than our Hebrew morph_segments.
- Sample, Gen 1:1 (verified word-for-word): ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεὸς
  τὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ τὴν γῆν, each word with lemma, G number, gloss,
  transliteration.

## License — the honest picture

- The **repo's own LICENSE is MIT** — that covers CenterBLC's work
  (the TF conversion and added features) and is the operative grant
  for the dataset as distributed.
- The README declares the provenance chain openly: **CATSS** (Univ. of
  Pennsylvania) → **Eliran Wong's RLXX1935** → CenterBLC. CATSS data
  historically carries a user-declaration regime rather than a free
  license, and Wong's repo carries **no license file** (GitHub API:
  license = none). The MIT grant therefore sits on top of a layer
  whose own terms were never a clean open license.
- The Rahlfs 1935 edition is published by the Deutsche
  Bibelgesellschaft, which asserts rights in the edition; the ancient
  text itself is public domain, the edition's status varies by
  jurisdiction.
- `bol_*` features defer to the BibleOL project's licenses (EzerIT).
- **Verdict:** usable with attribution and a recorded provenance note —
  this is how other open projects (e.g. LXX Reader) use it — but it is
  **not as clean as OSHB (CC BY 4.0) or the Samaritan corpus
  (CC BY-NC 4.0)**. Kit's options: (a) accept under MIT + provenance
  note; (b) restrict to internal alignment research, nothing
  redistributed; (c) hold out for Swete (public-domain text, but no
  finished open morphology/Strong's — a much poorer dataset).

## Structural consequences (confirmed by the census)

- **Crosswalk is real work.** LXX Psalms = 151 chapters / 2,533 verses
  (MT: 150 / ~2,461, with the one-psalm offset); Jeremiah 52 chapters
  but 1,298 verses (MT 1,364 — the LXX text is shorter and reordered);
  Daniel exists twice (Dan 406 verses vs DanTh 424).
- **Book choice for Daniel:** DanTh (Theodotion) is the form quoted by
  the NT and printed in most LXX editions; Dan (Old Greek) is the
  older translation. A build must pick one as primary or carry both.
- **Book order differs** from MT (e.g. Micah before Amos in the
  dataset's sequence) — our book table needs an LXX order column or a
  mapping, not an assumption.
- Brenton's English (1844, public domain) remains the planned bridge
  for any computed Greek→English layer; its machine-readable
  availability was **not** re-verified in this audit.

## Recommendation

Accept CenterBLC/LXX under its MIT license with the provenance chain
recorded wherever we record OSHB/SP attribution, and build the
**witness layer first** (verse-level LXX presence beside SP/DSS/Syriac,
then word-level alignment via the CATSS-style Hebrew–Greek parallel) —
the dataset's per-word G numbers, glosses, and transliteration make a
full Greek interlinear track a later extension of the same load, not a
second project. Canon scope (MT-only vs full Greek canon) and the
Daniel choice are Kit's rulings, needed before the load is designed.
